Positioning is the place you hold in a client's mind, against your competitors. It answers four questions:

  1. What is your business?
  2. Who are you talking to?
  3. What problem do you solve for them?
  4. What makes you unique?

01The business

Their business: more than eight sites in ten state it.

84% of websites clearly say what the company does. Consulting firms, far less often: 58%.

What it meansThe business is the easiest point to state. Every competitor states it too. So it sets no one apart.

02The target

Seven in ten do not clearly say who they serve.

37% do not say what type of client they serve. 34% address everyone: “companies”, “organisations”. 29% name a precise target.

What it meansExamples of precise targets: “cosmetics brands”, “the pharmaceutical industry”, “retailers”. A prospect knows at once whether the site speaks to them.

03The problem solved

Four in five promise a benefit, without saying which.

81% mention a benefit, but in vague terms. 13% do not mention one. 6% clearly say what problem they solve.

What it meansThe whole industry could sign “making better decisions” or “driving brand growth”. A problem solved is stated precisely, from the client's point of view. For example: “knowing before launch whether the product will be chosen”.

04The difference

To stand out, the most common argument is seniority.

48% of French-language websites give the company's age: 25 years on median. Half the industry displays its seniority. So it sets no one apart. 23% of websites prove their difference with another fact.

What it meansA difference is proven by a fact a competitor cannot write. On the websites that prove it, that fact is a number eight times out of ten. For example: “120,000 surveys a year”.

05The words

From one site to the next, the same words.

“Accompagner” (to support) appears on 35% of websites, “clients” too. “Expert” on 30%, “stratégie” and “data” on 23%.

What it meansPut together, these words make a sentence any provider could sign. It says neither what the provider does, nor why to choose them.

Average score out of 4: 1.9 for tool vendors and media agencies, 1.7 for fieldwork and panels, 1.4 for research agencies, 1.0 for consulting firms.

What it meansResearch agencies make up half of the websites read. 3% of them clearly say what problem they solve. Consulting firms are below average on all four questions.

07Score out of 4

One site in two clearly answers only one question in four.

48% of websites clearly answer only one question: the business, nine times out of ten. 12% clearly answer none. 4 sites out of 299 answer all four.

What it meansThe business tells the prospect they are in the right place. The other three answers give them a reason to choose this provider over another.

08Abroad

Abroad, one provider in two proves its difference.

In France, one in four. Among 110 providers headquartered outside France, 50% prove their difference, against 23% in France. 15% clearly say what problem they solve, against 6%.

What it meansLarge international groups pull this 50% up. They can cite their global rank, their volumes, their countries.

Method. On 22 October 2026, we read the homepage of 361 market research providers based in France, taken from the mazeo database. An AI extracted the presentation text, then scored it the way a prospect would, on the four positioning questions: the business, the target, the problem solved, the difference. Each point is said clearly, said but vaguely, or missing, following the rules of lesson 1 of the growth academy. Words are counted on the 252 French-language websites.

Limits. 62 websites could not be scored: unreachable site, unreadable text or unusable AI score. Only the homepage is read. A website's score can be debated: anyone can see their own at the top of this page. Provider types: those of the mazeo database. Outside France: 110 websites scored with the same rules.
